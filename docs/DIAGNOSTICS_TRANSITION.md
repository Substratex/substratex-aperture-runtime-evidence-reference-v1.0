# Validation, Calibration & Diagnostic Transition

Aperture separates three questions that are often collapsed:

1. **Does the implementation behave deterministically and preserve the declared evidence contract?**
2. **Does a detector or measurement classify controlled cases as intended?**
3. **Does the measurement generalize scientifically across independent systems and domains?**

Historical conformance fixtures primarily support the first question. Calibration fixtures pressure-test the second. Neither, by itself, proves the third.

Healthy long-horizon systems can exhibit repetition, convergence, synchronization, fixed-point stabilization, tool reuse, and invariant orchestration without being unstable. Calibration must therefore distinguish productive recurrence from degenerative recurrence rather than treating novelty reduction as a universal failure proxy.

Scientific validation requires broader controls, falsification opportunities, prospective evaluation, comparative baselines, and independent replication.
